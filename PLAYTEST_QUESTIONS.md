# Live paper playtest: Last Straw runway and meaningful combat

Status: PROPOSED TEST / OPEN, not production. Recorded 16 October 2026 from Joseph's current request. No completed games or results recorded. This document does not change RULES.md, CARDS.json, DECKS.json, browser or simulator behavior. The October 14 baseline remains in NOTES.md; the proposal below is a later candidate paper-test overlay, not a locked replacement.

## Joseph's proposed experiment

- Reveal/trigger Last Straw when a Leader first crosses from above 5 to 5 or less Composure, instead of at 0.
- Remove the Last Straw Guard overlay for this experiment: Ready Characters do not globally prevent Trouble.
- Intent: a momentum shift with runway to use the Last Straw, without otherwise redesigning the game.
- Joseph also asks whether combat needs stronger incentives, whether Trouble interception or earlier Leader attack/blocking deserves testing, and whether a dedicated no-Trouble prevention Character role would help.

## Settle before shuffling: do not silently infer these rules

1. Does 0 now mean immediate defeat after a spent Last Straw, or does the existing additional successful Cause Trouble finish remain at 0? Recommendation for a clean runway experiment: reveal at <=5, keep counting actual remaining Composure, lose at 0 after resolving any newly triggered protected Last Straw. This is an assistant proposal, NOT Joseph's confirmed rule. If a single effect crosses both 5 and 0, Last Straw must resolve before deciding defeat; otherwise burst bypasses its purpose. Do not reset Composure to 5 or discard overshoot without an explicit test decision.
2. Keep forced end-of-turn on reveal? Recommendation: retain it for the first experiment, but observe its impact separately from the threshold. Existing timing normally prevents follow-up actions after the reveal.
3. Do universal Last Straw combat benefits begin at reveal and persist after recovery? Define an explicit state: simply changing the threshold does not automatically make the old 0-Composure state text work at 5.
4. How do recovery above 5, returning below 5, and empty-deck Last Straw work? Reveal remains one-time; do not grant repeated Last Straws. Keep empty-deck handling explicitly agreed rather than allowing an accidental new interpretation.
5. Review only the selected Last Straw proxies for incompatible text. Fake Your Own Death (set to 5, skip next turn) is materially different if you already reveal at 5. Take Everyone With Me ('drops to Last Straw') needs an agreed numerical/state meaning. Do not assume old rough concepts remain equivalent.

## Questions after each game

1. Did you enjoy it, and would you voluntarily play again? What was the best moment?
2. When did you feel meaningfully ahead or behind? Could the losing player name a plausible recovery line?
3. What was your actual Composure when Last Straw revealed, and did you get a useful turn afterward?
4. Did Last Straw create a consequential decision/momentum swing, merely postpone defeat, or decide the game automatically?
5. Was ending the opponent's turn itself stronger than the printed Last Straw effect? Did ending your own turn feel punishing if self-loss triggered it?
6. Was the hidden Last Straw reveal surprising in a satisfying way? Could the opponent play around it afterward?
7. Did an oversized Trouble effect cross both thresholds or consume the runway? Was resolution clear and fair?
8. Did you deliberately avoid triggering the opposing Breaking Point or Last Straw? Was that interesting planning or tedious threshold arithmetic?
9. Name an Attack you chose instead of Trouble. What future opposing pressure, engine or resource did it remove? If no Attack was worthwhile, why not?
10. Did you leave an opposing threat alive to race instead? Did that choice work, and did either player care about the other's board?
11. Did staying Ready protect something valuable or enable an ability, or was it usually a wasted action without Guard?
12. Did Florida Man's delayed Breaking Point have a board left to affect, and did its payoff actually matter? Did HOA's Breaking Point influence which Character caused Trouble?
13. Did the winner prevail through board control, Trouble racing, a threshold swing, or an unavoidable hand/effect finish? Did decisions change that outcome?
14. Did you have a visible, affordable way to stop the decisive threat on your previous turn? Separate 'none existed' from 'I missed it' and 'I spent it earlier.'
15. Did either player feel unable to interact, unable to make progress, or stuck doing an obvious action? What precise board caused that feeling?
16. Which rule/card caused confusion? What single change would you test next, and which moment is evidence for it?

## Required observation: cheap swarm and lack of universal retaliation

Joseph explicitly requested this stress test after the initial questionnaire. This is a test priority, not approval to change combat rules or a completed result.

Concern: a board of Cost-1/2 Characters may concentrate several Attacks into exposed expensive Characters without taking return damage, preserve its attackers, and Cause Trouble with remaining eligible bodies. Conversely, fewer expensive Characters may have enough Power to defeat any small Character but lack enough actions to contain the swarm. Test both directions; combat might be too profitable for the swarm, not merely unattractive relative to Trouble.

During the paper games, deliberately look for a cheap-wide board versus a smaller expensive board. Record the actual cards, Ready/Rotated states, damage and relevant abilities at one representative turning point. If the existing matchup does not produce this situation, mark the concern UNTESTED; a later focused legal deck/setup test needs explicit agreement rather than silently altering the current five-game decks.

Respect the baseline: ordinary Attacks target Rotated Characters, new arrivals normally cannot Attack or Cause Trouble immediately, each ordinary Attack Rotates its attacker, damage persists, and Retaliate is already a printed exception. Do not let the same Character Attack then Cause Trouble without an explicit Ready effect. Multiple attackers can concentrate persistent damage, but cannot ordinarily attack a protected Ready target.

Post-game questions:

- Did Cost-1/2 attackers repeatedly remove expensive Characters while surviving? How many attackers/actions and total resource investment did each removal require?
- How much Trouble did the swarm forgo to clear the board, and how much did its unused eligible Characters still cause that turn?
- After the expensive side retaliated on its own turn, did the swarm retain or cheaply rebuild a decisive body-count advantage? Was its limit Power, available actions, resources or cards in hand?
- Did playing a large Character offer a credible stabilisation line, or did using it merely expose it to a safe multi-attacker takedown? Was staying Ready meaningful protection or a losing concession?
- Did printed Retaliate, durability, removal or other actual tools help at an accessible cost? Distinguish unavailable answers from missed or previously spent ones.
- Was swarm strength conditional on commitment and risk, or was flooding cheap bodies the obvious plan regardless of the opponent?
- At the recorded board, which cheap attackers would have been Defeated or damaged if defenders dealt their Power back? Would that create a meaningful decision or simply make expensive Characters oppressive? Label this a hypothetical unless separately played.

Conditional follow-up: if repeated no-return-damage trades produce a self-reinforcing swarm, propose a separate controlled comparison of baseline combat versus universal retaliation. Keep decks and the agreed Last Straw overlay unchanged, swap pilots/first player where practical, and specify retaliation timing (including defeated defenders) before playing. Existing Retaliate must not deal return damage twice. No such alternate combat rule is approved by this note. Compare attacker survival, remaining Trouble, ability to stabilise and enjoyment; also check whether retaliation overcorrects against small Characters. Do not assume extra Trouble blocking fixes safe combat trades.

## Conditional follow-ups: BANKED pending results

- Last Straw still gives no usable runway: inspect overshoot, effect choice and turn-ending protection before changing the threshold again. Earlier reveal is not automatically a meaningful turn.
- Combat rarely matters: check whether removing a Character prevents enough future Trouble/engine value to justify foregoing Trouble now. Prefer isolated stat/deck/incentive tests before replacing the core with Leader attacks and blocking.
- Staying Ready is pointless: inspect relevant Ready abilities/protection and threat exposure. Do not add a universal reward solely to make all three actions equally frequent; distinct roles may choose differently.
- Unanswerable Trouble racing persists: test ONE accessible prevention/interception design against the no-Guard version, not a simultaneous blanket blocking overhaul.
- Prevention causes lockouts: cap the amount or number of prevented Trouble events, add a real commitment cost, or make the preventing Character exposed and removable through ordinary combat. Test duplicates, Ready loops, protection stacking and alternate removal access.
- Thresholds dominate ordinary play: inspect automatic turn ending, self-trigger incentives and swing strength before adding more comeback systems.
- Selected Last Straw cards become nonsensical under the overlay: revise only proxy wording for that test and record it; do not rewrite the shared pool without results.

## Dedicated no-Trouble Character role: exploratory, not a new type

The existing rules already support Characters with 0 Trouble, which cannot Cause Trouble unless raised to 1+. A new formal Character type is not required. A no-offence controller can make combat relevant, but unconditional global Trouble denial while Ready risks protecting the very card the opponent must remove: ordinary Attacks cannot target Ready Characters.

Candidate for a later separate test, not an approved card: during your turn Rotate this Character to stand watch until your next turn; prevent the next 1 Composure loss caused by an opposing Character's Cause Trouble, once during that interval regardless of how many Ready/Rotate cycles occur. The prevention commitment exposes the Character to ordinary Attacks. Exact wording, cost, stats and stacking remain OPEN. Prevention of Composure loss would NOT stop the production baseline's special final successful Cause Trouble at 0; this interaction must be consciously chosen if that finish remains. Limiting one loss event is less absolute than shutting down all Trouble, but may be too weak or encourage token sequencing; test before tuning.

Questions: Did it buy a useful turn? Did the opponent attack it, bait its protection or race through? Was there a real cost to fielding it? Was it a dead draw, mandatory include or oppressive in multiples? Does a nominal 0-Trouble Character gain offensive Trouble through buffs? Was the resulting interaction funny and satisfying or simply a tax?

This overlaps the existing BANKED Trouble-denial/School Administration ideas in NOTES.md. Do not promote their keyword names, Leader or global denial into production. No additional cards, keywords, balance changes or core blocking system are approved here.
